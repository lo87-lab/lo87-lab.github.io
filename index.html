The Study Box

High School Engineering Capstone · Team Project

The Study Box was a year-long senior capstone project created by our team. We explored difficulties students can experience when trying to study while managing phone distractions. Our concept was a lockable phone box designed to encourage focused study sessions and more structured study habits.

The Challenge

Explore how distractions and difficulty managing study time can interfere with a student's ability to focus on schoolwork.

Our Proposed Solution

A lockable phone compartment paired with a visual timer and a proposed study-session routine.

Team Collaboration

We researched the problem, compared ideas, reviewed concept sketches, and considered feedback while developing our design.

My Contribution

I contributed concept art and helped create a physical prototype as part of the team.

04 /

Step One — Research the Problem

Our team began by researching difficulties children and teenagers can experience when studying. We focused on phone distractions, procrastination, task initiation, time management, and maintaining attention.

Understanding Study Habits

We explored how distractions can make it harder to begin a task, manage study time, and maintain focus.

Survey Research

Our group created separate surveys for adults and children or teenagers to gather perspectives on study habits.

Survey Responses

We gathered 107 student responses and 117 adult responses through our survey outreach.

Existing Products

We researched existing study products to compare their features and explore what our concept might combine in one design.

Design question: How might we help students set aside phone distractions and follow a planned study session?

05 /

Step Two — Final Concept & Features

The concept sketch communicates the proposed overall appearance of the Study Box and the features our team considered during the design process.

Lockable Phone Compartment

Provides a dedicated place to store a phone during a planned study session.

Visual Timer

A proposed timer helps users see their study interval and planned break.

Camera Housing

Holds the proposed camera component and provides a defined position for the camera's viewing area.

Ventilation & Electronics

The design considered ventilation and space for electronic components. These features required appropriate design and testing.

06 /

Step Three — Onshape Parts

Our Onshape technical drawings show individual components through dimensioned views and isometric views. They help communicate the geometry, openings, dimensions, and intended relationships between parts.

Why technical drawings matter: A sketch communicates the idea, while dimensioned drawings provide information about geometry, size, and component placement.

07 /

Step Four — Companion App Concept

Our app concept was planned as a companion to the Study Box. The proposed interface explored study-session controls, progress information, and a way to coordinate with the physical product.

Focus Session

The proposed interface explored a 25-minute study period followed by a 5-minute break.

Progress View

A planned feature for displaying study-session progress and helping users review their routine.

Connection & Unlock Code

The concept explored connecting the app to the device and using an unlock-code system after a session. These were proposed features.

Purpose of the App

The goal was to support the physical product with a digital interface for managing study sessions.

08 /

Step Five — Materials & Components

Our material research considered durability, heat resistance, cost, and repeated mechanical use. The following were researched options for the proposed product.

Enclosure Materials

Polycarbonate was considered for the enclosure because of its impact resistance and heat tolerance. ABS was also discussed as an alternative.

Moving Hardware

Stainless steel was considered for selected hardware, such as springs and hinges, because of its corrosion resistance and suitability for repeated movement.

Electronic Components

The proposed system included a Raspberry Pi 5, display, buttons, status lights, camera, and electronic latch.

Manufacturing

Our team explored injection molding as a possible method for manufacturing the plastic enclosure, considering fit, openings, and tolerances.

09 /

Step Six — Prototype & Improvement

01 · Prototype

Building a Physical Model

Our team worked on a physical prototype and considered component placement, dimensions, openings, and how parts could fit together.

02 · Review

Identifying Testing Needs

Our planning identified areas requiring evaluation, including heat management, latch reliability, camera coverage, and durability. This does not mean every test was completed.

03 · Feedback

Considering Design Changes

After reviewing the project, one idea was to explore a fisheye camera lens instead of a rectilinear lens to capture a wider field of view.

What I learned: Engineering involves research, design decisions, technical communication, collaboration, and refining ideas in response to feedback.
